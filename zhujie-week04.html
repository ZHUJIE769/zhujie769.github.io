Exercise 04A: Constraints

Date: October 15, 2026

Research

  • Adobe — A5 Paper Size Guide

    This guide gives the A5 dimensions, 148 × 210 mm, and discusses its use for flyers. The small format makes the order of information important.

  • Adobe Illustrator — Printer’s Marks and Bleeds

    This guide explains trim marks and bleed. It relates to preparing a flyer for print and keeping essential information clear of the trim edge.

Process Work

My brief is to promote a newly opened pie cafe using one side of A5, two colours and Arial. I have no budget for stock images. For the cafe scenario, the name is the pie room, opening on 15 November 2026, daily from 10:00 to 20:00. The opening-week offer is 20% off from 15–21 November.

The flyer uses a large name, a geometric pie drawing and a prominent discount. The opening date and hours sit below the offer. Cream and cherry wine keep the design within two colours.

My 20 Design Constraints
  1. A5 finished size. Keep the composition within 148 × 210 mm.
  2. One side. All required information must fit on one face.
  3. Two colours. Use only cream and cherry wine in the model.
  4. Arial. Use Arial for all text, including the wordmark study.
  5. No paid stock budget. Draw original imagery or use permitted supplied material.
  6. Cafe logo. Make the name identifiable as a wordmark.
  7. Opening date. Place the date where it is easy to find.
  8. Opening hours. Keep the hours readable at the printed size.
  9. Discount. Make the offer clear without hiding its limits.
  10. New pie-specialist cafe. Communicate the type of business.
  11. Clear reading order. Choose a primary, secondary and supporting message.
  12. Safe margins. Keep essential content away from trim edges.
  13. Small-print legibility. Avoid reducing details until they are unreadable.
  14. Offer period. Give the proposed promotion a clear duration.
  15. Accurate information. Check spelling, dates and numerical consistency.
  16. Single consistent wordmark. Do not change the cafe name across versions.
  17. Simple original drawing. Keep the pie icon feasible to draw independently.
  18. Limited production time. Agree a realistic making and checking schedule.
  19. Paired decisions. Discuss choices and record both contributions.
  20. Clear final export. Prepare a readable image or PDF at the correct size.

Final Outcome

Reflection

I can see how a few clear choices work within a limited brief. Arial and two colours keep the information organised. The large discount catches attention, while the date and hours give practical details. I do not need to fill every space to communicate the offer.

Exercise 04B: Reverse Workflow

Date: October 15, 2026

Research

  • Design Council — Framework for Innovation

    The Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver stages offer a way to think about design decisions. I use this as a process comparison while working backwards from a finished flyer. My reverse analysis is an interpretation of the visible result.

Process Work

I use the finished the pie room flyer as my study object. I separate what is visible from the earlier decisions that can be inferred. I work backwards from the final file to the original brief.

Final Outcome

  1. 07

    Finished flyer

    I start with the name, offer, pie graphic, opening date and hours.

  2. 06

    Checking and export

    The file needs checks for size, content, type and colour.

  3. 05

    Layout

    The name and discount lead the page; supporting details follow.

  4. 04

    Type and colour

    Arial provides the type system; cream and cherry wine limit the palette.

  5. 03

    Ideas

    Possible arrangements need to balance the main message and the details.

  6. 02

    Research and content

    The audience, promotion and required information shape the choices.

  7. 01

    Brief

    The starting point is to promote a new pie cafe within the given limits.

Reflection

Working backwards helps me look beyond the final appearance. A date or offer needs to be clear before it can be placed in the layout. The type and colour choices also affect how much information fits. I find this useful for understanding the order of tasks and where a change could affect later work.

My Weekly Journal

The cafe brief gives me a small space and clear limits: A5, one side, two colours and Arial. The flyer puts the discount in a large block, with the opening date and hours below it. This keeps the offer easy to find. I can see how a simple shape and a few type sizes can organise the page without paid stock images.

The reverse workflow looks at the same design from the other direction. I start with the visible result, then work back through checking, layout, type and colour, ideas and the brief. These earlier stages are an interpretation of the finished graphic. This helps me understand how one decision affects the next.
