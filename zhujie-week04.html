Promote a newly opened pie cafe using A5, two colours, Arial and no paid stock imagery.

Open the 20 proposed constraints
  1. A5 finished size. Keep the composition within 148 × 210 mm.
  2. One side. All required information must fit on one face.
  3. Two colours. Use only cream and cherry wine in the model.
  4. Arial. Use Arial for all text, including the wordmark study.
  5. No paid stock budget. Draw original imagery or use permitted supplied material.
  6. Cafe logo. Make the name identifiable as a wordmark.
  7. Opening date. Place the date where it is easy to find.
  8. Opening hours. Keep the hours readable at the printed size.
  9. Discount. Make the offer clear without hiding its limits.
  10. New pie-specialist cafe. Communicate the type of business.
  11. Clear reading order. Choose a primary, secondary and supporting message.
  12. Safe margins. Keep essential content away from trim edges.
  13. Small-print legibility. Avoid reducing details until they are unreadable.
  14. Offer period. Give the proposed promotion a clear duration.
  15. Accurate information. Check spelling, dates and numerical consistency.
  16. Single consistent wordmark. Do not change the cafe name across versions.
  17. Simple original drawing. Keep the pie icon feasible to draw independently.
  18. Limited production time. Agree a realistic making and checking schedule.
  19. Paired decisions. Discuss choices and record both contributions.
  20. Clear final export. Prepare a readable image or PDF at the correct size.
  • Use Arial for the name, offer and practical details.
  • Use cream and cherry wine as the two colours.
  • Use a simple geometric pie drawing instead of paid stock imagery.
  • Make the offer prominent, then place the opening date and hours below.
  • Keep the selected model image unchanged, as requested by zhujie.

Final Outcome

Reflection

Draft for review.

The flyer model shows how a few clear choices can work within a limited brief. Arial and two colours make the main information easy to organise. The large discount catches attention, while the opening date and hours give practical details. This is an AI-assisted classroom model, so I would describe what the model demonstrates rather than claim a hand-drawn or paired process that is not recorded.

Exercise 04B: Reverse Workflow

Date: October 15, 2026

Swimming against the tide: start with a finished object and investigate the stages that may have produced it.

Research

Proposed study object: the completed digital teaching flyer shown in 04A. This is an available graphic to analyse, rather than an invented student outcome. The brief also permits a work, object, painting, event or logo; replace this choice if the pair selects another object.

Evidence sources are the visible model and the supplied Week 04 brief. The diagram distinguishes observable facts from inferred stages. A diagram is the chosen presentation format, while staged reverse research is the core requirement.

Process Work

  1. With a partner, identify what is directly visible in the finished graphic.
  2. Ask which decisions and dependencies would have been necessary.
  3. Work backwards from export and checking to layout, testing, concepts and brief.
  4. Label what is known, what is inferred and what further evidence would be needed.
  5. Replace the proposed stages with findings from the pair’s actual research.

Reflection

Draft for review.

Working backwards helps explain the decisions behind a finished design. In this model, I can observe the text, colours and layout, but the earlier stages are inferred. The diagram separates these two kinds of information. It is useful for understanding the order of research, layout, checking and export without pretending to know the exact history of the original making process.
