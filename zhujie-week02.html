My Work Ethic / Personal Journal

The most important things in my design work are respect, honesty and responsibility. I want to listen to clients and understand what they need, while also thinking about the people who will see the design. A design should attract attention, and the information should be clear and true.

I am still learning Illustrator, Photoshop and Canva, so I need to be honest about my skills. I should ask when I am not sure and accept useful feedback. Being independent is important to me, but I also need to communicate well with other people.

Originality also matters to me. I enjoy trying different styles and want to develop my own ideas. I would not copy someone else’s work or use images and fonts without checking permission. In the future, I hope to work in a creative studio and eventually run my own studio. These principles can help me work with others and take responsibility for my decisions.

Exercise 02B: Costing & Ethics

Date: October 6, 2026

Consider whether to accept a project and explain the boundary behind the decision.

Research

The three scenarios come from the course brief. They are hypothetical, not claims about named real companies. Assess the stakeholders, persuasive purpose and potential consequences before choosing a position.

  • Landmine manufacturer: a promotional website.
  • Fizzy drink: high sugar, dubious chemicals and a “fun” positioning.
  • Jeans company: exploitative labour practices in developing countries.

Final Outcome

Combined draft / 79 words

I would decline the landmine website because promotional design could help sell products intended to harm people. I would only consider the sugary drink packaging if the client accepted clear ingredients and responsible communication, without misleading health claims or targeting children. I would decline the jeans hangtag while exploitative labour practices continued, because attractive branding could conceal harm to workers. My decisions would depend on evidence and the client’s willingness to make meaningful changes, rather than on appearance alone.

Expanded scenario notes, if needed
01

A landmine manufacturer

Proposed decision: Decline

A website can increase the credibility and reach of a manufacturer. In this scenario, that persuasive work would support a product intended to harm people. My proposed boundary is to decline the promotional project. A separate project about victim support or humanitarian clearance would need its own brief and evidence, rather than being treated as equivalent work.

02

A sugary fizzy drink

Proposed decision: Conditional

A playful package can attract attention while obscuring important information. I would only consider the project if the client allowed clear product information and responsible claims. I would avoid presenting the drink as healthy or directing misleading messages at children. If the client insisted on concealing concerns, I would decline. This is a proposed ethical stance to review.

03

A jeans company using exploitative labour

Proposed decision: Decline while practices continue

A hangtag is small, but it contributes to how a brand earns trust. I would not want a polished label to make exploitation less visible. I would decline while the practices continued. A different decision would need credible evidence of meaningful changes and transparent communication; a new slogan alone would not resolve the concern.
