My Ethics Journal

The most important things in my design work are respect, honesty and responsibility. I want to listen to clients and understand what they need, while also thinking about the people who will see the design. A design should attract attention, and the information should be clear and true.

I am still learning Illustrator, Photoshop and Canva, so I need to be honest about my skills. I should ask when I am not sure and accept useful feedback. Being independent is important to me, but I also need to communicate well with other people.

Originality also matters to me. I enjoy trying different styles and want to develop my own ideas. I would not copy someone else’s work or use images and fonts without checking permission. In the future, I hope to work in a creative studio and eventually run my own studio. These principles can help me work with others and take responsibility for my decisions.

Reflection

I want to make original work, listen to feedback and keep learning. Being honest about what I can do matters because I am still developing my skills. I also need to think about the audience and the effect of my design, especially when a message could be misleading or harmful.

Final Outcome

Scenario 01: A Website for a Landmine Manufacturer

I would not accept this project because I would be uncomfortable using my design skills to promote products intended to cause injury. A website can improve a company’s credibility and help it attract customers, so my contribution would have consequences beyond appearance. I would explain my decision respectfully rather than judge the individual contacting me. I would prefer to support work related to mine clearance, survivor assistance, or public education, where my communication skills could serve a purpose that matches my values.

Scenario 02: Packaging for a Sugary Fizzy Drink

I would consider this project only if the client agreed to honest communication. I would ask for verified ingredient information and a clear explanation of the intended audience. The description of “dubious chemicals” would need clarification rather than an assumption that unfamiliar ingredients are harmful. I would not hide sugar information or create unsupported health claims. A playful design could be acceptable if it did not mislead consumers. If the client insisted on deceptive messaging, I would decline the job.

Scenario 03: A Hangtag for an Exploitative Jeans Company

I would not accept this project while the exploitative labour practices described in the scenario continued. A hangtag contributes to the brand’s image, and I would not want my work to make unfair treatment seem acceptable. I would explain my concern and ask whether the company was taking meaningful, independently checked steps to improve conditions. A new visual identity alone would not address the problem. I would reconsider only if there were credible changes, rather than promises made for marketing purposes.

Reflection

These scenarios show that accepting a project is also a professional decision about values. My response should consider the purpose of the work, the information available, and the people affected. I can ask questions and suggest changes, but I also need boundaries when a client expects misleading communication or asks me to support practices that conflict with my principles.

My Weekly Journal

For me, good design should attract people without misleading them. My ethics statements focus on respect, originality and honesty. I want to listen to clients and accept feedback, but I also need to keep my own judgement. Being independent does not mean ignoring other people.

The three scenarios help me explain my boundaries. I would refuse the landmine project and work connected with ongoing labour exploitation. For the drink packaging, I would first ask for reliable information and honest communication. I do not think unfamiliar ingredients should automatically be treated as harmful. My decision needs to consider the people affected, not just the appearance of the design.
